Education Manager: From Educational Need to AI-Assisted Workflow

I connected experience in educational leadership with hands-on software engineering to develop an AI-assisted communication prototype for educators, taking the product concept through the technical evolution of an initial implementation.

The Challenge

Teachers frequently need to communicate sensitive information to students’ families. These messages require clear writing, appropriate tone, and alignment with institutional guidelines.

Drawing on my experience in educational leadership, I identified an opportunity for an AI-assisted application that could help teachers prepare communications using relevant school documents. The objective was to support teachers’ judgment, not replace it.

My Role

I originated the product concept. A collaborator developed the initial implementation; I subsequently took over the project and worked on its technical evolution.

My work included:

  • Updating software dependencies.
  • Diagnosing and addressing application and service problems.
  • Establishing a functioning connection to a live AI service.
  • Improving the integration between product workflows and technical components.
  • Exploring the product’s commercial potential.

Architecture and Technical Decisions

The implemented architecture combines a Next.js frontend for educator and administrator workflows with a Fastify backend API. PostgreSQL and Prisma support persistent application data. Firebase provides authentication and hosting, while backend services are hosted on Google Cloud infrastructure. AI integration sits behind the backend API.

Institutional sources and bounded retrieval

School administrators can manage institutional document sources and their versions. Educator workflows use eligible active sources, with local, bounded retrieval of eligible passages to provide context during generation.

This retrieval is not production semantic search or provider-backed RAG. It provides a limited document context within the prototype’s workflow; it does not establish verified school-policy compliance.

Drafts that remain editable

The workflow supports draft editing, revision history, and source references. These features support teachers as they review and refine communications using the available institutional context.

What We Achieved

The project reached a working, cloud-hosted demonstration with:

  • Authenticated educator and administrator workflows.
  • Structured communication drafting.
  • Management of institutional source documents.
  • Draft generation and revision history.
  • Live AI integration.
  • Automated tests and deterministic generation paths for repeatable verification.

These are prototype capabilities. Commercial adoption and measurable improvements in teachers’ productivity have not been demonstrated.

Limitations and Lessons

The application remains a prototype. Live AI integration has had operational limitations involving service availability and restart handling. The complete system is not production-ready.

The project illustrates why useful AI software requires workflow design, access controls, institutional document governance, and predictable application behavior, beyond a connection to an LLM.

Leadership Perspective

Academic leadership helped me identify and understand the educational problem. Software engineering experience helped me translate that problem into an architecture, a working implementation, and concrete technical decisions.

The value of this work lies in connecting domain knowledge with hands-on execution: understanding what educators need, taking technical ownership of an existing implementation, and making practical decisions about the prototype’s capabilities and limits.
