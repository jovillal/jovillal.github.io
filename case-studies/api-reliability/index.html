Improving API Reliability Through Engineering Standards

As Head of Engineering, I worked with the development team to strengthen software quality and make production releases more reliable at a financial technology platform.

The Challenge

A financial technology platform experienced frequent production disruptions following software deployments. Recovery required substantial emergency intervention, diverting engineering resources from planned development.

The underlying problem extended beyond software defects: inconsistent testing, code reviews, and deployment discipline made releases unnecessarily risky.

My Approach

As Head of Engineering, I worked with the development team to introduce a more disciplined approach to software quality.

We established shared standards:

  • Mandatory code reviews.
  • Automated tests integrated into development workflows.
  • Passing tests as a prerequisite for progressing with pull requests.
  • Controlled production deployments rather than direct, unreviewed changes.

These were team agreements. Technical enforcement was introduced progressively, particularly in established core services; adoption and enforcement were not uniform across all projects.

Results

75%+ test coverage
Automated unit-test coverage increased from almost zero to over 75% across seven microservices in approximately six months.
Approximately one incident per month or fewer
Production disruptions fell from occurring after nearly every deployment to approximately one incident per month or fewer.

More predictable engineering delivery

Shared quality expectations and reduced emergency recovery work helped make engineering delivery more predictable.

Constraints and Trade-offs

The improvements took place under significant delivery pressure and with a small engineering team.

New projects were often assigned to individual developers, limiting opportunities for independent code reviews and comprehensive enforcement.

We prioritized core production services and introduced safeguards progressively. The same practices were not adopted across all projects.

Leadership Perspective

Reliable software requires more than automated testing. It depends on shared standards, technical safeguards, team commitment, and pragmatic allocation of limited engineering resources.

The central transformation was organizational: moving from reactive incident management toward a more disciplined engineering culture.
